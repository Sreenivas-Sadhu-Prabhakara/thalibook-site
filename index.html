01 · The problem

You cook for 300.
The maths cooks you.

A caterer's real job starts after the "yes". You quoted by the plate, took the advance — now you're on the floor with a calculator working out how much paneer, how many kilos of rice, how much oil for this headcount, this menu. Every event, from scratch, by hand.

🧮

The shopping list is redone every time

Same dishes, new headcount — so you re-multiply every ingredient by hand. Miss a dish and you're back at the market at 6am on function day.

📒

Quotes live on scraps of paper

Headcount, per-plate rate, which advance came in for which event — spread across a diary, WhatsApp and your head. Nothing adds up in one place.

🗓️

Two functions in one week = double the chaos

Shopping for a wedding and a griha pravesh separately means two market runs and two chances to over-buy onions you already had.

💸

Balances slip after the advance lands

The advance came in, the event happened — and the balance follow-up quietly never went out. Money left on the table, every season.

02 · The insight

A catering quote is just headcount × per-plate, and the shopping list is fully determined by the menu and the headcount — yet both get re-calculated by hand for every single event.

ThaliBook's whole job is to close that gap: put the per-plate quantities in a recipe book once, and let every booking compute its own market list — Σ(per-plate qty × headcount) across the menu — so the buying is exact, and a busy week rolls up into one run.

03 · How it works

Quote, book, buy. The list is a by-product.

  1. 1

    Create the event and quote it

    Client, date, event type, headcount and a per-plate rate. The quote — headcount × per-plate — is shown live as you type. No calculator.

  2. 2

    Build the menu from the recipe book

    Pick dishes — paneer butter masala, chicken biryani, dal tadka, veg pulao, chole, naan, gulab jamun. Each already carries its per-plate quantities.

  3. 3

    Record the advance and the event is booked. ThaliBook computes the ingredient shopping list automatically: every dish × headcount, summed and rolled into kilos and litres.

  4. 4

    Shop the week, chase the balance

    A calendar of upcoming events, a consolidated this-week market list across every booking, and a one-tap balance reminder. Nothing falls through.

Where does the shopping list come from?

From your menu. Every dish in ThaliBook's built-in recipe book carries per-plate ingredient quantities. When you book an event, ThaliBook multiplies each ingredient by the headcount and sums it across all the dishes, then rolls grams up to kilos and millilitres to litres — so the market list is exact, not a guess.

My dishes and portions are my own. Can I change the recipes?

Yes. The recipe book ships with common Indian dishes and sensible per-plate quantities to get you moving, but the per-plate amounts are yours to tune. Adjust a dish once and every future event that uses it gets the corrected shopping list automatically.

Do I have to take the advance through the app?

No. Take the advance however you already do — cash, UPI, bank transfer — and record the amount in ThaliBook. Recording it is what confirms the booking, generates the shopping list, and starts tracking the balance due. ThaliBook doesn't force a payment rail on you.

I run several events in a week. Do I shop for each one separately?

No — that's the point of the this-week list. ThaliBook consolidates every booked event in the next seven days into one combined market run, so you buy 14 kg of onions once instead of chasing it event by event.

I'm not very techy. How long to set up?

An afternoon. The recipe book is already loaded, so you just add an event, pick dishes, and enter the advance. The demo opens with a full board of Bengaluru catering events already computed, so you can see exactly how it works before you type a thing.

What does it cost?

Pricing is worked out on a quick discovery call and billed monthly on subscription. We're keeping public prices off the page while we onboard our first caterers.
